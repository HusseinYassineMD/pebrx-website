Our Mission & Meaning

A biotechnology company developing precision therapeutics and diagnostics for APOE4 brain inflammation in Alzheimer’s disease and related disorders.

Who we are

PebRx began with a question: why does APOE4, a gene variant carried by up to 25% of the U.S. population, make the brain so much more vulnerable to Alzheimer’s disease?

In pursuing that question, our scientists traced APOE4’s effects to brain inflammation and to an enzyme at its center, cPLA2. In APOE4 carriers, cPLA2 is hyperactivated, driving a lipid inflammation pathway that can progress for years before symptoms appear and before irreversible damage occurs.

That finding defined our mission: to intercept the disease at its molecular root, rather than respond after memory loss begins. PebRx is developing novel therapeutics and diagnostics to detect and treat APOE4 brain inflammation. Our lead program, the BRI-Series of brain-penetrant cPLA2 inhibitors, targets this pathway directly. Paired with companion PET imaging to identify and monitor patients, BRI-Series is the first step in a pipeline built to bring precision, disease-modifying therapies to the largest untreated genetic risk group in Alzheimer’s disease.

The company was co-founded by Hussein Yassine, MD, Stan Louie, and Vsevolod “Seva” Katritch, PhD, and is led by CEO Amy Wang, PhD, MBA.

Our approach

A new approach is needed for APOE4 carriers: detect inflammatory damage earlier, and treat it at the pathway that drives it.

  1. 01

    APOE4 raises the stakes.

    Up to 25% of the U.S. population carries APOE4, the strongest common genetic risk factor for Alzheimer’s disease. Brain changes can begin 15–20 years before cognitive symptoms, and APOE4 carriers face a higher risk of vascular injury, including cerebral amyloid angiopathy and amyloid-related imaging abnormalities, on current anti-amyloid therapy.

  2. 02

    Inflammation sits upstream.

    PebRx’s platform centers on cPLA2, an enzyme whose hyperactivation in APOE4 carriers drives inflammatory lipid signaling. That pathway is linked to blood-brain barrier disruption, synaptic dysfunction, tau pathology, and neurovascular injury — damage that starts before memory loss.

  3. 03

    BRI-Series targets that pathway.

    The BRI-Series is a set of brain-penetrant, selective cPLA2 inhibitors designed to reduce pathological activation while preserving normal signaling. Companion PET tracers, including 18F-FDHA and 18F-AA, are being developed to select patients and monitor the pathway in the living brain.

  4. 04

    The same biology reaches further.

    Beyond Alzheimer’s disease, PebRx sees future potential for cPLA2-targeted therapies where blood-brain barrier disruption and lipid-mediated inflammation play a pathological role, including traumatic brain injury, stroke, acute neuroinflammatory disorders, CAA in Down syndrome, and sporadic CAA.

How we’ve advanced

From the first evidence that APOE4 activates cPLA2 to a selective inhibitor series and companion PET imaging, the program has moved through peer-reviewed papers and translational funding.

  1. 2022

    APOE4 linked to cPLA2

    Molecular Neurodegeneration paper showed APOE4 activates cPLA2 and implicated that pathway in neuroinflammation.

  2. 2023

    PET probe for brain lipids

    Preclinical evaluation of 18F-FDHA, a PET probe for brain DHA uptake, published in ACS Chemical Neuroscience.

  3. 2025

    $8 million NIH grant

    USC collaborators received NIH funding to develop a selective cPLA2 inhibitor for brain inflammation in APOE4 carriers.

  4. 2026

    BRI-Series reported

    Discovery of the BRI-Series published, alongside papers on cPLA2 in synaptic pathology, cognition, and vascular inflammation.

  5. Today

    Pre-IND BRI-Series

    Lead program advancing through discovery and IND-enabling work, with companion PET tracers in development.
